The most responsible AI may be the smallest one that works

A decision framework for using AI where it creates real value, choosing the lightest capable system, and keeping people accountable for the result.

2026-09-06

All articles

A better first question

Do we need AI here at all?

AI is often introduced as a feature before the problem has been described. Reverse that order. Write down the decision, the people affected, acceptable error, and the value of a correct result. Then compare AI with a search box, a rule, a spreadsheet, a database query, or a clearer process.

A deterministic tool is easier to test when the task has stable rules. A language model becomes useful when language is genuinely variable, outputs can be reviewed, and occasional uncertainty is acceptable.

  • What exact task will the system perform?
  • What happens when it is confidently wrong?
  • Can a person meaningfully review the output before harm occurs?
  • What data must leave the device or organization?
  • What simpler method has been tested as a baseline?

The physical system

Digital intelligence still uses material infrastructure.

Data centres consume substantial electricity, and AI is one driver of growth in that demand. There is no single honest footprint number for every AI request because energy use changes with model, hardware, response length, workload, data-centre efficiency, and electricity supply.

The useful response to uncertainty is measurement and proportionality rather than treating every request as either harmless or wasteful.

Right-size the system

Use capability, context, and computation deliberately.

Start with the smallest model or service that passes a task-specific evaluation. A compact local model may be enough for classification, extraction, or repetitive formatting. Retrieval can supply current bounded information without training a larger model.

Caching repeated answers, limiting unnecessary context, shortening outputs, batching work, and turning off unused experiments can reduce both cost and computation.

Keep responsibility human

A fluent answer is not an accountable decision.

Human review only works when the reviewer has enough time, evidence, authority, and subject knowledge to disagree with the output. A decorative approval button is not meaningful oversight.

For consequential uses, record the source data, model or version, significant instructions, reviewer, and final decision so errors can be investigated and corrected.

A small policy

Five rules a team can use tomorrow.

Responsible AI begins by acknowledging limits of evidence, attention, energy, and authority. A well-chosen small system can leave more room for all four.

  • No AI feature without a named problem, owner, and success measure
  • No sensitive data without a documented need and approved handling path
  • No high-impact output without meaningful human review and an appeal route
  • No oversized model when a smaller tested system meets the requirement
  • No permanent deployment without monitoring, incident reporting, and a way to switch it off
