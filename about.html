About Guang

Technology, nature, and learning by building

I’m Guang. I like solving practical problems, understanding the systems behind them, and keeping notes I can share. GLab is where I explain the projects I work on, the guides I find useful, and the lessons I don’t want to forget.

What I build, what I’m learning, and why technology should work well with the world around us

What keeps me curious

Technology and nature can coexist harmoniously

I’m passionate about building a future where technology and nature coexist harmoniously. I enjoy exploring cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, sustainable farming, networking, and hands-on engineering projects.

I believe in using technology thoughtfully to create positive change for people, communities, and the planet. That means asking practical questions as well as technical ones: does a tool solve a real problem, can people maintain it, what data does it need, and are its trade-offs worth it?

Where the work comes from

The network-operations work has a practical starting point. The Noney BSNL Broadband NMS brought service workflows, network information, mapping, and follow-up into one operational view. It is now retired, and I present it as a case study—not as a live service. The problems behind it still shape the questions I work on: how to keep information connected, make system status clear, and spot failures early.

The rest of GLab is made up of smaller tools, network and server experiments, practical guides, and notes from studying. I also make room for community technology, digital preservation, and sustainable farming. Useful technology includes the people and places around it—not only the screen it runs on.

What you’ll find here

  • Project stories: why a project exists, how it changed, what is currently available, and what is no longer running.
  • Practical guides: steps, prerequisites, expected results, references, and common limitations.
  • Learning notes: concepts I’m working through, questions I’m testing, and useful ways to explain a topic.
  • Community-minded work: projects such as Rongmei Hymnal, where access, preservation, and careful handling of material matter.

How I try to document the work

I don’t want a project page to look more complete than the project itself. If it is archived or still in development, I say so. If I haven’t tested something, I don’t call it tested. When a detail can change—software behaviour, prices, security advice, or a service feature—I try to link to a source and say when I checked it.

I use AI tools to help organise a question or get a first draft moving, but I still have to check the result. I would rather leave a gap visible than fill it with an anecdote, test result, or claim I can’t support.

Where I’m taking the work

GLabNetOps is the name I use on this site for my broader network-operations and tooling work. It is a public project track, not a claim that every related tool is part of one active production system. The GLabNetOps overview explains the background and current boundaries; separate case studies show the status of the Noney NMS, Network Analyzer, and SSH Fleet Doctor.

I’m also continuing to document Rongmei Hymnal, an offline-first digital library that brings community song collections into a searchable reading space. Its content deserves careful review and attribution as well as thoughtful software.

What stays private

GLab is a public notebook, not a place to expose operational details. Project pages can explain the purpose, high-level design, decisions, limitations, and public resources without publishing private source code, passwords, tokens, customer records, internal network topology, or live administrative endpoints. Screenshots will only be added when they are safe to share.

Browse or get in touch

Start with the project case studies, read the articles and guides, or browse all my public GitHub repositories ↗. If a page is unclear or you find a mistake, use the contact page and include the exact page, passage, and any evidence that helps reproduce the issue.

Please don’t post credentials, customer information, private keys, or sensitive operational data in a public issue or message.
