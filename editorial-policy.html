How GLab is maintained

Editorial policy and corrections

GLab is a personal publishing space for project documentation, practical guides, reflections, and learning notes. The aim is to explain how something works, what evidence supports a claim, what remains uncertain, and where a reader can verify the details.

Project status and evidence

Project pages distinguish active, in-development, completed, retired, and archived work where the repository supports those labels. A documented feature is not necessarily a feature verified in the current deployment. Test summaries include the context and limits of the test when known. I will not claim field results, production performance, or historical events that the available records do not establish.

Sources and updates

Time-sensitive statements—such as software capability, model availability, security guidance, prices, and regulatory information—should link to official or primary sources and be checked close to publication. A page’s update date means substantive content was reviewed or changed; it should not be used to imply that every external fact is continuously monitored.

Evidence, voice, and AI-assisted work

I may use AI to organize notes, compare explanations, identify research questions, draft alternatives, or edit for clarity. I remain responsible for the published claims and wording. AI output is a draft, not evidence: verify factual and technical statements against reliable sources or actual tests before publication. Remove stock introductions, repeated conclusions, vague claims, and contrasts that do not add reasoning. Keep specific trade-offs, constraints, and uncertainty. Vary the structure to suit the subject; do not add personal anecdotes merely to make a page sound more human or pad a short answer to meet a word count.

Voice matters as much as polish. These pages should sound like notes from my actual work and learning, not a generic explainer that could belong to anyone. Start with the problem or question, explain the reasoning and trade-offs, and be specific about what I know, what I checked, and what remains uncertain. Do not add first-person claims when there is no real experience to describe. Avoid stock introductions, repeated summaries, and tidy conclusions added only to complete a template. A short, direct note is better than a long passage that says little; a reference guide can stay factual without pretending to be a personal story.

Publication review

Before changing an article to published, use the editorial review checklist. Release checks validate page structure and metadata; they cannot judge whether an article has a real point, a credible voice, or enough evidence. That review remains a deliberate human decision.

Technical guides and security

Guides should state prerequisites, scope, expected results, limitations, and relevant safety boundaries. Cybersecurity and network assessment instructions are for systems the reader owns or is explicitly authorized to test. Commands should be inspected before execution, especially when they require elevated privileges or affect remote systems.

Corrections

If you find a factual error, broken link, unclear instruction, attribution issue, or concern about published material, use the contact page or open an issue in the relevant public repository. Include the page URL, the specific passage, and a reliable source or proposed correction where possible. I will review the report and update the page when a correction is warranted.

Content rights and community material

Software licences do not automatically grant rights to accompanying text, songs, photographs, artwork, or other media. Community and cultural material should retain appropriate attribution and source context. Where rights or wording are uncertain, the page should state the uncertainty instead of guessing.

Limitations

GLab is maintained as a personal project. Pages may become outdated as software, services, and standards change. Before relying on instructions for a consequential decision, consult current official documentation and independently verify the steps in an appropriate environment.
