# Editorial review checklist

Use this before changing an article's `glab:article-status` from `draft` to `published`. This is a human review, not an AI detector or a style score. AI-assisted writing can be good; non-AI writing can still be vague or wrong.

## Purpose and value
- [ ] Can I state the reader benefit in one sentence?
- [ ] Does the page add specific evidence, analysis, documented experience, a useful comparison, or synthesis beyond generic advice?
- [ ] Have I removed repetition and paragraphs that only restate the headline?

## Evidence and accuracy
- [ ] Can I trace factual and technical claims to sources, repository records, measurements, or tests?
- [ ] Are time-sensitive claims checked close to publication?
- [ ] Do I distinguish what I verified from what I inferred, recommend, or have not tested?
- [ ] Are commands, examples, links, expected results, project status, and dates accurate?

## Voice and specificity
- [ ] Does the article start with the actual problem or question rather than a broad slogan?
- [ ] Are first-person claims true?
- [ ] Have I removed invented anecdotes, vague “lessons,” inflated claims, and filler?
- [ ] Does the structure fit the subject rather than follow the same formula as every other article?
- [ ] Are hypothetical examples labelled as such?

## Balance and limits
- [ ] Are important trade-offs, edge cases, failure modes, and uncertainties explained?
- [ ] Have I avoided making plans sound shipped, demos sound like production proof, or general rules sound universal?
- [ ] Is the length justified by useful information rather than a target word count?

## Publication and safety
- [ ] Are citations, attribution, image rights, and alt text handled?
- [ ] Are credentials, customer data, internal hostnames, sensitive topology, and unredacted screenshots excluded?
- [ ] Do the title, excerpt, canonical URL, dates, and structured metadata match?
- [ ] Have I run the full site check and browser smoke tests where relevant?

**Decision:** Publish only when the article is useful, accurate for its stated purpose, and honest about its limits. If it fails, narrow the claim, add evidence, revise it, or keep it as a draft. Do not use arbitrary “human-written percentages” or AI detector scores as acceptance criteria.
